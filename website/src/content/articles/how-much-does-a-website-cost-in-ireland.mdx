It's the first question almost everyone asks us. Fair enough. A website is a real investment, and the prices you see online are all over the place. One company says €800. Another says €25,000. Both might be telling the truth.

So here's the honest version. What a website costs in Ireland in 2026, what pushes the price up or down, and the questions worth asking before you sign anything.

## The short answer

Across the Irish market, you'll typically see something like this:

- **DIY or template builders:** a few hundred euro a year. You do the work.
- **A small brochure site from a freelancer or small studio:** roughly €2,500–€6,000.
- **A bespoke marketing site from an agency:** roughly €6,000–€15,000.
- **An online shop:** roughly €8,000–€25,000, depending on products and integrations.
- **Custom builds with bookings, calculators or customer areas:** €20,000 and up.

Treat those as ballparks, not quotes. Which brings us to the real answer.

## Why nobody can give you a price on the first call

Our founder Cathal O'Reilly is blunt about it. Quote too early, he says, and it "could be too expensive… or else it could be too cheap."

A price given before anyone understands the job is a guess. Too high and you walk away from something that would have paid for itself. Too low and the corners get cut later, usually on the bits you can't see. That's why we scope first and quote against a written plan. You know exactly what you're getting, and so do we.

## What actually drives the cost

**1. How many page types, not how many pages.** A site with 40 blog posts isn't 40 times the work. A site with eight different page layouts is. Templates get designed once and reused.

**2. Selling online.** Checkout, stock, shipping, payments and product data add real work. On a platform like Shopify much of it comes built in, which keeps costs sensible. We've written a [plain guide to Shopify in Ireland](/latest/shopify-ireland-or-wordpress/) if you're weighing it up.

**3. Integrations.** Booking systems, CRMs, quote calculators, stock feeds. When we built the new [Modern Tyres](/work/modern-tyres/) site, customers needed to go from a reg number to a booked fitting at one of 80 depots in a few minutes. That's a very different build to a five-page brochure site, and it's priced like one.

**4. Content and photography.** Somebody has to write the words and supply the images. If you've got them, great. If not, budget for it. Good copy and real photos do more for enquiries than almost anything else on the page.

**5. Moving from an old site.** A proper migration maps every old URL to its new home so you keep your Google rankings. Skipping it is false economy. We've seen businesses lose half their traffic overnight because nobody set up redirects.

**6. The platform.** WordPress, Shopify or custom code all cost differently to build and to run. More on that below.

## The cost nobody mentions: after launch

The build price is only half the picture. Ask any agency what it costs to keep the site running and make changes once it's live.

A lot of web companies make most of their money on support. Every small change becomes a ticket, and every ticket becomes an invoice. Cathal is clear that we don't want to work that way:

## WordPress, Shopify or custom code?

The platform changes both the build cost and the running cost.

- **WordPress** suits content-heavy sites and teams who already know it. Cheaper to start, but plugin updates and security patches are an ongoing job.
- **Shopify** is usually the right call for online shops. You pay a monthly plan plus apps, but checkout, payments and hosting are handled.
- **Custom code** is what we build most often now for marketing and service sites. Fast, very little upkeep, and nothing to patch.

It's a real shift for us too. A year ago almost every site we built was WordPress. Today it's around 30–40%. As Cathal puts it, "our ability sometimes was restricted by the platform… the sky is the limit." We compare all three, side by side, on our [website design and build](/services/website-design-and-build/) page.

## Five questions to ask before you sign

1. **What exactly is included?** Pages, templates, copy, photos, training, redirects.
2. **Who owns the site and the content when it's done?**
3. **What does it cost to make a change after launch, and how long does it take?**
4. **How will you protect my Google rankings if I'm moving from an old site?**
5. **How will we know it's working?** Enquiries, sales or bookings, not just "it looks nice".

If an agency can't answer those plainly, keep looking.

## Can you get help paying for it?

Possibly. Small Irish businesses can apply for the Trading Online Voucher through their Local Enterprise Office, which covers up to half of eligible costs to a ceiling of €2,500.
